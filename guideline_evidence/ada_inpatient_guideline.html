Section 16: Diabetes Care in the Hospital | Clinical Practice Recommendations

EVIDENCE-BASED INPATIENT GLYCEMIC TARGETS & REGIMENS
Recommendation 16.4: Inpatient Glycemic Targets
Insulin therapy should be initiated for treatment of persistent hyperglycemia starting at a threshold ≥ 180 mg/dL (10.0 mmol/L) (checked on two occasions).
Once insulin therapy is started, a target glucose range of 140–180 mg/dL (7.8–10.0 mmol/L) is recommended for the majority of critically ill and non-critically ill hospitalized patients. [Level of Evidence: A]
Recommendation 16.5: Preferred Inpatient Insulin Regimen (Basal-Bolus vs Bolus Alone)
A regimen consisting of basal insulin plus prandial (mealtime) rapid-acting insulin and correction doses (Basal-Bolus Regimen) is the preferred standard of care for non-critically ill hospitalized patients with adequate nutritional intake. [Level of Evidence: A]
⚠ Strongly Discouraged Practice: Bolus / Sliding-Scale Alone
Sole reliance on sliding-scale insulin or rapid-acting bolus insulin alone (e.g., Novorapid without basal coverage) in the inpatient setting is strongly discouraged. This approach fails to control overnight and fasting hepatic glucose output, resulting in severe fasting hyperglycemia (GDP elevated) and wide glycemic fluctuations. [Level of Evidence: A]
